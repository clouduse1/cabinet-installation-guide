What to Expect During a Kitchen Cabinet Installation

By the Editorial Team | Published: July 2026 | Last Updated: July 2026

The Day the Kitchen Becomes a Kitchen

Cabinet installation is the moment a kitchen renovation stops being demolition and dust and starts looking like a kitchen again. It is also the phase homeowners understand least - they choose the cabinets, then a crew arrives and, some days later, the boxes are on the walls, level and aligned. What happens in between follows a precise, repeatable sequence, and knowing that sequence turns an anxious wait into an informed one. Understanding what to expect - the order of operations, the preparation that must precede it, and the coordination that must follow - is what lets a homeowner recognize good work as it happens and spot problems early. The detailed, step-by-step version of that process is laid out in the kitchen cabinet installation process; this primer maps the whole experience around it.

Before Anything: The Room Must Be Ready

Cabinets cannot go onto a wall that is not prepared, and the readiness work happens before installation day. Old cabinets are removed, walls are repaired and often painted (at least the areas that will be hidden), flooring decisions are settled, and the rough-in for plumbing and electrical is positioned for the new layout. Critically, the installer needs to know where the wall studs are and whether the walls and floor are reasonably flat and square - because cabinets are heavy, must be anchored into framing, and must end up level regardless of how uneven the room is. A room that is not ready turns installation day into a delay.

The Installation Sequence

Professional cabinet installation follows an order dictated by physics and practicality. The high points, roughly in sequence:

Step What happens Why the order
Layout & high point Level lines snapped; floor's high point found Everything references these lines, not the floor
Upper cabinets first Wall cabinets hung to the level line No base cabinets in the way to reach over
Base cabinets Set, leveled with shims, joined together Level bases are the counter's foundation
Fillers & scribing Gaps to walls closed and fitted Walls are never perfectly straight
Doors, drawers, hardware Fronts hung and adjusted; pulls installed Alignment is the visible quality signal
Countertop templating Counters measured against set cabinets Counters can only be templated once bases are final

The two details that most surprise homeowners are that uppers go up before bases, and that the floor is largely ignored in favor of a level line - because a floor is rarely level, and hanging cabinets to it would telegraph every dip. The whole install references gravity, not the floor.

Why Level and Scribe Matter So Much

Two words define quality cabinet installation: level and scribe. Cabinets that are not perfectly level produce doors that swing open or shut on their own, drawers that don't sit flush, and - worst of all - countertops that cannot be installed correctly on top of them. Scribing is the craft of fitting cabinet edges and fillers to walls that are never truly straight, so the finished run looks seamless rather than showing tapering gaps. These are the details that separate a professional installation from an amateur one, and they are invisible when done right and glaring when done wrong.

What Comes After the Boxes Are Up

Installation does not end when the cabinets are anchored. The countertop process begins only after the bases are set and level, because counters are templated to the actual installed cabinets, not the drawings - a fabricator measures the real run, then returns days later to install the finished tops. After counters come the backsplash, the plumbing and appliance hookups, and the final adjustments to doors and drawers. Understanding that the cabinets are one stage in a coordinated finish sequence - not the finish line - is what keeps expectations realistic about when the kitchen is truly done.

Recognizing a Good Installation

A homeowner does not need to be a carpenter to judge the result. The signs of quality are visible: cabinet doors aligned with even gaps (called reveals) around and between them, drawers that open smoothly and sit flush, cabinet runs that are level and plumb, filler pieces fitted tightly to walls with no tapering gaps, and no visible fasteners or damage. The signs of trouble are equally visible: doors out of alignment, gaps to the wall, cabinets that rock or feel loose, and drawers that catch. Knowing what good looks like - and that so much of it depends on the installer's skill and the preparation beforehand - is the homeowner's best protection, and it starts with understanding the full installation process from the ground up.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does kitchen cabinet installation take?

For a typical kitchen, the cabinet installation itself often takes a few days, though it varies with the size and complexity of the layout. That window covers hanging and leveling the boxes and fitting fillers; the countertop, backsplash, and final hookups happen afterward on their own timelines, so the full kitchen finish stretches well beyond install day.

Do upper or base cabinets get installed first?

Uppers first, in professional practice - hanging the wall cabinets before the bases are in place means the installer isn't reaching awkwardly over base cabinets and countertops. It surprises many homeowners, but it is the standard and more precise sequence.

Why don't installers just follow the floor?

Because floors are almost never level, and hanging cabinets to a sloping floor would leave counters out of level and gaps everywhere. Installers snap a level reference line and set everything to that, shimming the bases up from the floor's high point so the finished run is true regardless of the floor.

What is scribing?

Scribing is fitting a cabinet edge or filler strip precisely to a wall that isn't straight, trimming it to follow the wall's contour so the joint looks tight and seamless. Since walls are never perfectly flat, scribing is what makes a professional installation look built-in rather than approximate.

Can countertops be installed the same day as cabinets?

Generally no - countertops are templated (measured) against the actual installed and leveled base cabinets, then fabricated and installed days later. The cabinets must be final before the counter can be measured, which is why the counter is a separate, later step.

How do I know if my cabinets were installed well?

Look at the reveals - the gaps around and between doors should be even; drawers should sit flush and glide smoothly; runs should be level and plumb; fillers should meet walls tightly without tapering gaps; and nothing should rock or feel loose. Even gaps and smooth operation are the clearest signs of quality.

What has to happen before installation day?

The old cabinets removed, walls repaired and readied, flooring decisions settled, and plumbing and electrical roughed in for the new layout, with studs locatable for anchoring. A room that isn't prepared turns installation day into a delay, so the prep work is as important as the install itself.
